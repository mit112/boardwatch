# Architecture review — 2026-09-05 (product-level, against job-apps)

A read-only review done on `main` at `51d25cca`, between the merge and run 4. It deliberately does
NOT re-open the 2026-09-04 review (D-467, 26 code-level findings, executed) or the 2026-09-06
report's five findings. Those were defect hunts. This one asks a different question: **why does the
apply lane hold 3–5 of 40 leads at a 44% unapplyable rate while job-apps ships 20–40 a day at 16%,
and what does "done" have to mean for that to change.** Evidence came from two read-only passes
(job-apps at `~/dev/Job apps`; boardwatch's verdict → delivery path), the program docs, and `git`.
Every claim below carries a file or a decision number; nothing was measured on the live store.

## The one-paragraph answer

D-071 agreed a two-stage eligibility design: a deterministic, precision-first engine that ABSTAINS,
plus "a cheap agent-lane final gate over the ranked shortlist to recover the recall the deterministic
stage abstains on" (D-074). Stage one shipped and is excellent. **Stage two shipped as a manual CLI
handshake, held zero rows for four weeks, and is still not on the unattended path** —
`pipeline/runner.py` contains no LLM import or call. job-apps' whole precision edge is that its
judge IS on the daily path (`claude -p` headless, Sonnet, ~2 calls per posting, on the
subscription), with regex demoted to candidate-signal generation. Everything else in this review is
downstream of that one missing scheduler.

## Findings, in the order they matter

### 1. The designed second stage is not running, and T34 measures it for the wrong job

- `runner.py`: zero references to `gate_handshake`, `final_gate`, `extract_llm`, or `llm/`.
  `build_client` is reached only from `cli/eligibility_cmd.py`. The gate is `gate request` → a
  human-driven judge → `gate apply`, and nothing schedules it (STATE item 2, undecided).
- D-074's stated purpose was **recall recovery**: turn abstains into `eligible` with an evidence
  chain. T34's cadence rule (D-474 choice 4) counts only apply-lane `ineligible` — it measures the
  gate as a precision filter and would leave it manual if it "catches nothing", even though its
  designed job is to make the apply lane bigger than five. **M3's exit (apply lane ≥ 10) has no
  other lever on the page**: the deterministic engine cannot manufacture `eligible` from a JD that
  states a 2-year bar, and the years ruling is owner-gated.
- job-apps (`eligibility/judge.py:74`, `resume_tailor/model.py:26-40`): `judge_folder` calls a
  headless `claude -p --output-format json --safe-mode --tools "" --max-turns 4`, model
  `claude-sonnet-4-6`, six workers, a verdict cache keyed on policy/prompt/model version, and a
  provenance check that every `move` reason quotes a JD substring. Low-confidence or unverified
  moves quarantine to `review` — never auto-reject. That is boardwatch's keystone, already
  implemented on the other side, running every day.

**Recommendation.** Put the existing handshake on the daily path over the delivered slate, before
tailoring (see 3): a `gate` stage in `run_pipeline` that writes the request, drives the judge
headless the way job-apps does (no API key needed; the `eligibility-judge` skill's blind protocol
is the prompt), applies verdicts through `record_gate_verdict`, and **fails open per D-074** —
judge unavailable ⇒ the slate delivers exactly as today. Cost is ~40 judgments per run. Keep
T34, but read it for both directions: apply-lane `ineligible` caught AND review-lane `uncertain`
released. The second number is the one M3 needs.

### 2. Seniority is read from the title only; the largest defect class has no reader

- 28 of 77 blind-judged apply-lane defects are seniority mismatch (senior body under a junior title).
- `rules.yaml` has no seniority family. `rank/seniority_gate.py::seniority_verdict(title, …)`
  classifies the title against `leveling.yaml` and is used by `top`/`show`/reports only — it is
  **not wired into `delivery/review_gate.py::classify`**. `role_gate`'s `_DENY_EXEC_RANK` is
  title-only. Nothing reads "own the architecture", "mentor junior engineers", "founding
  engineer" from a body.
- job-apps reads both: `job_discovery.py:353 check_seniority` (body years < 2 overrides a senior
  title; no years ⇒ title keywords) and `eligibility/evidence.py:121-133 _SENIORITY_PATTERNS`
  (body-expectation language) as candidate signals for the judge, whose reason taxonomy includes
  `senior_level`.

**Recommendation.** Two steps, cheapest first. (a) Wire `seniority_verdict` into `classify`:
`above_band` ⇒ review, one gate, title-only, no engine change. (b) Let the daily judge (finding 1)
carry body seniority through its existing reason taxonomy. Do NOT add a body-seniority family to
the catalog first: D-438 shows a new family is never verdict-neutral, so it needs corpus sizing,
and (b) gets the same read without moving `rules_hash` or restarting a freeze.

### 3. Tailoring runs before the lane split, so ~35 of 40 PDFs per run are built for the review pile

- `runner.py:1914` `leads = list(ranked.visible)` — the whole top-N (40 via the plist's `--top 40`,
  D-366) is tailored in the loop at `:1957`. The apply/review split (`review_gate.lane`) runs later,
  at queue sync (`delivery/queue.py:410`), and only chooses the folder.
- Per-lead tailoring is 4.35–64.05 s (plist comment; the driver is JD richness), so 3–40 minutes
  per run go into PDFs for leads Mit is told to skim, not apply to.

**Recommendation.** Split, then judge, then tailor: eagerly for the apply lane, lazily for review
(on promotion — a web-app action or `boardwatch tailor <id>`). This makes finding 1 cheaper
too: judge 40, render ~10–15. **B2 ("leads with a compiled PDF: 100%") must then be read as
apply-lane leads.** That is Mit's wording to change, not ours; it is flagged, not assumed.

### 4. The ranker never reads the verdict

- `rank/heuristic.py::score_posting`: skill 0.50, title 0.25, recency 0.15, location 0.10
  (`core/settings.py:29-35`). `top_cmd.py:561` sorts on that total alone; `:573-581` drops
  `ineligible` before the cut. `eligible` and `uncertain` tie, and with ~3.7k postings cut on
  rank alone every run (PROGRAM §1), an `eligible` posting can be buried under `uncertain` ones
  that scored higher on recency.

**Recommendation.** Verdict as a tier, not a weight: `eligible` first, then `uncertain` with
`role=swe`, then the rest — each tier sorted by the existing score. Rank-only, engine-neutral.
Confirm before building that rank weights are outside `rules_hash` (they are not eligibility
inputs, so they should be).

### 5. Neither bar measures the thing Mit compares against

- **B1–B7** measure "works unattended". B1 (≥ 10 net-new leads/day) is met ~9.6x and PROGRAM §1
  itself says a 14-day B1 pass is "close to guaranteed for the next ~92 runs by ledger drain
  alone".
- **Gate 1** measures per-source recall through a `(company, title)` key that RETIREMENT-PLAN §1
  documents as many-to-one in both directions, on aggregator hosts where identity can never
  match, and the plan's own answer for the largest source is "accept the loss" (D-453). The
  thresholds are unset; as written, Gate 1 can be argued indefinitely.
- What Mit actually said the switch-off test is: **20–40 positions a day** (2026-08-22), and
  "will boardwatch be as good as or better than job-apps at filtering?" — answered at 44.2% vs
  16.2% (2026-08-30).

**Recommendation.** Add one product bar and make it the completion test:
**B8 — apply-lane leads/day ≥ 20, blind-audited unapplyable ≤ 16% on n ≥ 100, over the 14-day
confirm.** Set Gate 1's per-source thresholds at what the retirement plan already concludes
(direct-ATS ≈ 100%; LinkedIn = accepted loss; Indeed = the lane exists) so the gate closes rather
than lingers. "Done" is then B1–B8 for 14 frozen days, and nothing else.

### 6. The process is now a first-class cost, and the roadmap already knows it

Measured with `git`, last 14 days, `main`:

| `src/` | 168 | 28,072 | 2,539 |
| `tests/` | 171 | 46,318 | 1,641 |
| `docs/program/` | 180 | **79,819** | **52,989** |

133 of 317 commits touch no code. `METRICS.md` is 12,826 lines, `STANDING-FACTS.md` 2,710, the
DECISIONS index 114 KB. The tree is 89k source lines and 130k test lines for one tenant;
`profile_bundle/` is 22.4k lines (25% of `src`) and `extract/` is 273. ROADMAP's own read:
"what the last four sessions bought is confidence, not yield". The CLAUDE.md rule "write STATE
ONCE, at the end" is not being followed — three of the last five commits are STATE rewrites.

**Recommendation.** No deletion. Two habits: METRICS gets one table row per run and one per
session, with prose going to the REPORT/HANDOFF files that are read once; STATE is edited once per
session as a diff, not rewritten. And an explicit scope call for Mit: **done = single-tenant
complete**, with multi-tenancy (P2 item 8, the generalized bundle) as a v2 goal, so the
generality tax stops being paid on every change to a product one person runs.

### 7. Smaller, for the record

- **Dedup granularity.** job-apps groups `(company, title)` refined by location at 0.95/0.85
  confidence (`job_index/dedup.py:88`); boardwatch dedups on canonical `job_id` only. 14–18%
  duplicates were measured pre-reset; T28 found 0 in 40 post-reset. Watch, do not build yet.
- **Experience-years exceptions.** job-apps except-lists `0-N`, `up to N`, `degree or N years`,
  `N internships` before treating a number as a floor (`eligibility/evidence.py:224-247`). The
  09-04 review fixed "no less than" and "18 months"; whether `rules.yaml` handles the four
  job-apps exceptions was not checked here and is worth one corpus probe.
- **What boardwatch does better, so it is not lost:** posture (no JobSpy Indeed scraping, ATS-direct
  lanes), the evidence chain on every verdict, the ledger and quarantine drains, body-quality
  preflight (`lanes/quality.py`), `delivery/server.py`, and 9,400 tests. job-apps is 39.6k lines
  in 117 files with none of that; its edge is one scheduler and one body-reading judge.

## What this means for the roadmap

M1 and M2 stand. **M3 gets its lever from findings 1–4**, which are one execution session:
gate stage on the daily path (fail-open), lane-before-tailor, `seniority_verdict` in `classify`,
verdict tiering in rank. They restart the freeze once, together, which is why they belong before
M2's provisional pass is relied on. M4 closes by setting the thresholds the plan already implies.
M5 runs B1–B8 for 14 days. Then boardwatch is called complete and job-apps is switched off.
